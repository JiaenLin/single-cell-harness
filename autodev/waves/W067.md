# W067 (PROF + HARN): what the maker cannot yet say, and what the fixture tier grades

**Registered** 2026-11-12 by the coordinator. ROUTE items R24 to R30 (the plugin line) and R02 (the maker). **Gaps**
GAP-C128, GAP-C131, GAP-C126, and the maker's part of GAP-C135. **Goals** A4.1 (0 of 9 today), A5.1, PROF.1, HARN.1.
**Design first** (RULES item 21a; the direction review's D5): a Fable agent writes the section "Design" below before
any builder starts. The next held-out plugin's blind grade does not wait for this design and is not shown to it
beforehand; the design is written from the three grades already recorded.

## Why
Three plugins have gone through the maker (the differential-expression, the cell-communication and the compositional
one), each graded blind first. scProfile's `main` holds all three and the dev suite ran on the cluster: one of the
three passes it. The other two do not, and not for a defect of their own:
1. **The compositional plugin's `agree` fails on three lines** (GAP-C128): its two figure sources order a factor's
   levels alphabetically, and one reference ratio is inverted on the fixture's second shape. The maker's design
   worksheet names no site for a level order, lets an answer add no statement, and cannot reach the model's frame, so
   the plugin's covariates and its interaction stay "named, not fitted". The fix cannot be made through the maker, and
   a plugin is never edited by hand.
2. **The cell-communication plugin's fixture tier fails by construction** (GAP-C131): the tier now accepts no refusal
   and requires every graded instance to end ok; on the synthetic fixture that plugin's own code ends `partial` on
   seven units (its database covers too few of the synthetic genes) and refuses one small sample. Nothing says how a
   partial, or a unit a plugin refuses for a declared reason, is graded.
3. **The inventory reads an empty plotting submodule as "no plots"** with a success exit (GAP-C126), so a wrapped
   package with seven plot methods was planned with none.
4. A plugin that asks a library for a worker count by hand is not flagged by the worksheet (from GAP-C135).

## The question the design must answer
What must the maker be able to SAY about a plugin, as a closed list of sites and verbs, so that the three known
failures are answers through the maker and the next plugin's are too: which of the recorded grades' failures are ONE
maker defect to fix once, and which are a plugin's own debt. And for the fixture tier: what does "passes the dev suite"
mean for a plugin whose correct behaviour on a small synthetic fixture is a partial result or a declared refusal: the
plugin declares, through the maker and before the run, the status it must reach on each fixture shape and why, and the
tier grades that declaration exactly; or the fixture grows what the plugin needs; or such a plugin cannot count.
Answered wrongly, A4.1 either counts plugins that do not work or can never count ones that do.

## What the design carries (item 21a)
The problem from the root (read the three blind grades and what each later maker change answered); THE CLASS for the
maker's sites (the closed list of things a plugin's code decides that the host's design must be able to set: the
reference level, a level order, a model frame, covariates, an interaction, a worker count, a figure source's columns;
the one place the list lives; the one test that walks it against every converted plugin) and for the fixture tier (the
closed list of statuses a graded instance may end in and what makes each a pass); the design by construction; one
other design and what it gives up; the build as waves of at most three numbered changes, per repository, each with its
acceptance check and proof-table rows, and with the spent plugins' outputs unmoved except where a wave says why; what
moves A4.1 for each of the three plugins and by which cluster job; the attacks; what the design gives up.

### 3. THE CLASS, part two: the fixture tier
| an instance ends | written at | it is a PASS of the tier when |
|---|---|---|
| `ok` | `out.json` (`scprofile/manifest.py`:55) | `ok` is what was declared for it (the default for every instance) |
| `partial` | `out.json`; set by the plugin (cellchat.py:4155) | declared for its unit kind with a quantity; the record carries that quantity, its value on the declared side of the floor, and the floor equal to the plugin's own config default; its tables exist |
| `refused` | `out.json` (`scprofile/plugin.py`:1388-1393; `_entry.py`:555, 604, 617) | declared for the unit a NAMED fixture hazard selects, and the refusal holds the declared phrase |
| `failed` | the run's status (`scprofile/status.py`:25; cli.py:1062, 5044-5059) | never |
| `unreadable` | cli.py:4252-4255 | never |
| `absent` | no instance ran (cli.py:4259-4261), or a declared unit left no record | never |
| `undeclared` (new) | a status outside the list, or two records for one unit | never |

A command that refuses or exits non-zero never passes: R46 stays (ladder.py:411-435). **The rule.** A plugin declares,
THROUGH THE MAKER (`sch dev edit --set on_fixture...`, one version bump) and before any run, the end each kind of unit
must reach on the fixture, and why. With no declaration every instance must end `ok`, as today: de and abundance declare
nothing. The ladder grades it EXACTLY, per shape and per instance: an instance that ends BETTER than declared fails too,
so a declaration is never a permission. The bounds, checked by the verb and by the ladder's first tier before anything
runs: (i) only `partial` and `refused` can be written, each with its reason; (ii) a `refused` names a key of the
fixture's own hazards that selects units (`fixture.HAZARDS`, fixture.py:122; today `tiny_sample`), so "everything
refused" cannot be declared; (iii) a `partial` names a quantity the HOST wrote (`ctx.partial`; `ctx.status` set to it by
hand is refused), a side, and the config key of its floor, and the fixture command passes no parameters; (iv) at least
one instance ends `ok`, and every instance not refused writes a table `agree` judges; (v) `agree` drops a refused unit's
products by unit, and a unit refused on one shape only is a disagreement (ladder.py:665-676). It cannot be bent after
the fact: the declaration is in the file at the pinned commit, its digest is in the tier's evidence, and `sch dev check
--predict` prints from it, WITHOUT running, the lines the gate job predicts.
**Should the fixture grow instead?** No, as a rule. The 7-cell sample is a hazard on purpose. The floor of 0.5 needs
every gene of at least 1,617 of 3,233 interactions (INFERENCE from ANATOMY:101 and cellchat.py:342: about a thousand
symbols of ONE tool's pinned database; the fixture holds 100 for 44 pairs, fixture.py:168-197), again for each
database-backed plugin, and a wider fixture moves de's and abundance's fixture tables and every baseline. It grows only
when NOTHING of a plugin can run on it (bound iv), as it once did (fixture.py:159-167). **What A4.1 counts under this
rule.** de: as today (no declaration; job 793345 read it PASS). abundance: it counts when part one takes its `agree` to
zero. cellchat: with `on_fixture` declaring sample and arm units `partial` on `database_coverage` under `min_coverage`,
the `tiny_sample` unit `refused` and the comparison `ok`, both fixture tiers pass exactly; it then counts on its
`agree`, which has NEVER compared anything (the tiers failed first, ladder.py:647-653).
